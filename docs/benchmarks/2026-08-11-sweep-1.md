## Findings

**1. `DTYPE=bfloat16` is the bug. fp32 is 20x faster.**

517.4 vs 25.9 tok/s at batch 1. The CPU flags explain it exactly: with no `avx512_bf16` and no
`amx_bf16`, PyTorch emulates every bf16 op, and the emulation costs far more than the fp32 path.

This fully accounts for production's 1.0 tok/s. bf16 tops out at ~3.9 GFLOP/s regardless of model
size, so at 494M params that predicts `3.9e9 / (6 x 494e6)` = **1.3 tok/s** — against 1.0 tok/s
measured in run 31431031730. The remaining gap is the data pipeline. Nothing else is wrong.

**2. The hardware is 5x better than the plan assumed.**

`docs/BASE_MODEL_PLAN.md` projected ~20 GFLOP/s. Actual fp32 is **78–100 GFLOP/s** — the 4-core
EPYC with oneDNN is far more capable than estimated, and the whole timeline compresses.

**3. Batching helps much less than predicted: 1.28x, not 3–6x.**

517 -> 663 tok/s from batch 1 to 32. At block 512, even batch 1 presents a 512-row GEMM, so the
matmuls are already large enough to saturate 4 cores; batching adds little. Batch 32 also costs
4.9 GB RSS for a 25M model. **Recommendation: batch 8, not 32** — 88% of peak throughput at half
the memory, leaving headroom for larger models.

## Follow-ups applied

- `run_config` now times the warmup step and, if it alone exceeds the budget, reports from that
  measurement instead of running another. A step cannot be interrupted once started, so without
  this a slow config overruns the budget without bound — `bf16/batch32` at ~20 min/step is what
  consumed the first sweep.
- bf16 is dropped from the default sweep (`--dtypes fp32`). Settled question on this hardware;
  re-checkable with `--dtypes fp32 bf16`.
- Job timeout 60 -> 90 minutes.
